## 4. Research Notes & Limitations

**Method:** Research was conducted across ten parallel research passes: six covering broad industry clusters (automotive, transportation/fleet, construction/trades, government/institutional, distribution/rental, and property/services) and four targeted follow-up passes (additional trades subcontractors, waste/environmental/industrial services, small-town Hamilton County coverage, and misc. fleet-heavy categories like couriers, marinas, RV dealers, and uniform/linen services), plus manual searches to fill specific data gaps. All passes used web search against official company websites, Google/Yelp business listings, BBB profiles, Chamber of Commerce and trade-association directories, FMCSA/carrier records, and news coverage. Results were consolidated, deduplicated by business name and address, and filtered against the verification checklist below.

**Filtering applied during compilation:**
- Every row required a real Hamilton County street address (not a PO box, not an out-of-county HQ) and a verified phone number. Well over 60 additional candidates surfaced across all research passes were **dropped** because a phone number or a resolvable street address could not be confirmed (small janitorial/cleaning companies, several funeral homes, freight brokerages, a security/crane/scaffolding company with only a service-area page, a portable-toilet company, a septic company, and others). They were excluded rather than included with guessed contact information.
- Candidates were also dropped for: conflicting/unverifiable data (e.g., a phone number that varied across sources with no way to confirm which was current), a firm headquartered in a neighboring county despite advertising Hamilton County service (e.g., a land surveyor based in Jasper/Marion County), a business flagged as possibly closed by one directory with no way to confirm it's still operating, and one collision-repair chain that itself performs glass replacement in-house (a competitor, not a prospect).
- Where the same business turned up independently in more than one research pass (e.g., a funeral-home chain location, a pest-control company, an appliance-repair shop under two different listed cities for the same address), it was merged into a single row rather than duplicated.
- "Estimated Fleet Size" is left as **"Not publicly verified"** for the large majority of businesses because no business publicly discloses an exact vehicle count. The 36 exceptions show a figure or description explicitly published by the company or a reputable secondary source (e.g., City Auto's ~889-vehicle inventory, Covenant Logistics' ~2,300 tractors per its public-company profile, Brown Bros.' 100+ employees, McKee Foods Transportation's DOT carrier registration). No fleet size was estimated or guessed.
- Emails are shown only where an address was actually published by the business (17 of 285). All others are marked "Not publicly available" rather than a guessed format.
- Decision-maker names are shown only where publicly attributed to a specific person by the business's own site, BBB, or another public record (29 of 285, mostly owners of smaller independent shops). Larger dealerships, corporate branches, and government departments generally do not publish an individual contact, and none was guessed.

**Environment constraint:** Direct page-fetching (visiting a company's website to read it live) was blocked by network restrictions in this research environment for most of the process; verification relied on indexed search-engine snippets of those same official sites plus independent directories (BBB, Yelp, Chamber, FMCSA/SAFER, corporate locators, news). This is a real but disclosed limitation — treat phone numbers and addresses here as search-engine-verified and cross-referenced, and do a final live check (call the number, confirm the site) immediately before outreach, as recommended for any prospecting list.

**What this means for next steps:** This is a strong, quality-controlled, county-wide starting list, not an exhaustive one. Categories most likely to still have additional un-surfaced prospects: very small independent trades contractors, commercial cleaning/janitorial firms, additional funeral homes, and small freight brokerages/owner-operator trucking outfits — all industries where many legitimate small operators exist without a strong web/SEO footprint. A follow-up pass with live site verification (rather than search-snippet verification) in those specific categories would likely add further volume without compromising accuracy.
